A no-prep activity for the restless afternoon, in under a minute.

Teatime Ideas is a phone app for older spouses caring at home for a husband or wife with dementia. It checks the time and the weather, and suggests something to start right now.

You’ve become the carer. The afternoons are the hard part.

“By four o’clock my husband is restless and I’ve run out of ideas.”
“I know she used to love the garden. I just can’t think what to do with her at half three.”

Both quotes are invented, written to show the problem. They are not real people.

Today, most carers in this position turn on the television, go for a drive, ring a family member, or keep trying things until something works. There is usually no plan ready for that hour.

Who it’s for

  • The segment: husbands and wives in their 70s and 80s, caring at home in Ireland for a spouse with moderate dementia.
  • Who uses it every day: the spouse who is the carer, on a phone or a hand-me-down tablet.
  • Who pays or decides: a family member or a carer-support funder.

How it works

  1. Tell it about them, once.Pick what they used to do for work or as a hobby, such as gardening, knitting or football.
  2. Open it in the afternoon.It checks the time and the weather, and picks an activity that suits.
  3. Start it, or swap it.Each idea lists what you need, and baking and gardening ideas come with the steps. Don’t like it? Tap once for another.

What it costs

€4 a month per household (assumption)

Free for the carer. The €4 would be paid by a family member, or covered by a carer-support funder. This price is an assumption to be tested, not a real offer.

What exists today

Two real tools that already do part of this. Both were checked on 28 Oct 2026.

  • MindMate. It offers brain games, a daily activity plan, exercise and recipes, aimed at people keeping their own minds active. Its homepage does not mention suggesting an activity for a carer to start with their partner, based on the time, the weather and their past hobbies.
  • Jointly, from Carers UK. It helps a circle of carers share messages, tasks and medication lists, and costs a one-off £2.99 per circle. Its page describes coordinating care, not suggesting something to do together during a restless afternoon.

What testing showed

A family member who is a spouse-carer looked through the page and tried the demo.

What they told me: three things were missing. There were only three favourite-thing categories. The page only mentioned husbands, though wives care for their husbands too. And the baking and gardening ideas had no steps.

What I changed: the page now speaks to husbands and wives. The demo now has eight favourite-thing categories with four ideas each, and every baking and gardening idea has numbered steps.
